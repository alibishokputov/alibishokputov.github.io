Academic Focus

Research

Dissertation

"Telework and the Changing Urban Constraint: Housing, Home-Work Separation, and Uneven Commute Exposure"

Advisor: Casey Dawkins  ·  University of Maryland

Traditional urban economic models treat commuting cost as the main force behind where households and firms locate. Widespread telework loosens that constraint, allowing residential and employment location to decouple for some workers while leaving others tied to place. The dissertation uses Maryland as a case study, combining statewide commuter survey data, administrative records, and spatial econometric methods to examine how this shift is redistributing housing costs, home-work separation, and commute exposure across the workforce.

Research Interests

Telework & Remote Work

Labor force participation, employer adoption, and distributional effects of remote-work access

Commuting Behavior & Transportation Equity

Travel demand, mode choice, and access gaps by income, race, and geography for workers who cannot telework

Housing & Climate Resilience

Capitalization of accessibility into land values; exposure of low-income renters to heat and flood risk

Neighborhood Change & Revitalization

Early prediction of blight risk and spatial contagion in distressed neighborhoods; equity-centered intervention design
