Research

Dissertation

"Reshaping Metropolitan Spatial Economic Structure in the Telework Era"

Advisor: Casey Dawkins  ·  University of Maryland

The dissertation investigates how the normalization of telework is redrawing the spatial logic of metropolitan economies. Traditional urban economic models treat commuting cost as the principal force organizing where households and firms locate. Widespread telework relaxes that constraint—potentially decoupling residential from employment location, redistributing VMT and emissions, and restructuring demand for transit and road infrastructure. The dissertation uses Maryland as a policy-relevant laboratory, combining statewide commuter survey data, administrative records, and spatial econometric methods to trace these dynamics empirically.

Research Interests

  • Telework and remote work — labor force participation, employer adoption, and the distributional effects of remote-work access
  • Commuting behavior and VMT — travel demand modeling, mode choice, and the implications of hybrid schedules for vehicle miles traveled
  • Transportation equity — access gaps by income, race, and geography; the adequacy of transit for workers who cannot telework
  • Housing and transportation investment — capitalization of accessibility into land values; evaluation of public programs and subsidies
  • Urban data science — spatial analysis, machine learning, and reproducible research methods applied to urban policy

Selected Findings

~⅓ of workforce

Hybrid workers constitute approximately one-third of Maryland's workforce and log the longest average commutes—a counterintuitive pattern with significant implications for VMT projections and transit investment. (Maryland Commuter Survey, 2022–2024)

<$160K of $1M

Less than $160,000 of Maryland's $1 million annual Commuter Tax Credit allocation was claimed despite legislative support—driven by employer administrative burden, unclear ROI, and limited transit coverage in program-eligible areas. (Commuter Tax Credit Evaluation, 2024)

Auto lock-in

Few Maryland auto commuters can switch to transit without substantially longer travel times, underscoring structural transit inadequacy that telework policy alone cannot resolve. (Maryland Commuter Survey, 2022–2024)
