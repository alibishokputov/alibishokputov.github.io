Research

My work asks how telework changes what distance costs, and who pays it. Fields: urban and regional planning, transportation, housing policy, and urban data science.

Dissertation

Expected Spring 2027

The Unequal Price of Proximity: Telework, Commute Frequency, and Place-Tied Workers in U.S. Cities

Committee: Casey Dawkins (chair), Chester Harvey, John Park

The monocentric model treats the commute as the price of cheaper or roomier housing at the city’s edge, with trip frequency held fixed. Remote work relaxes that assumption, turning frequency into a worker-specific choice and expanding locational freedom for those who can work from home while leaving others unchanged. The dissertation traces this uneven repricing of distance across three essays, showing that remote work has redistributed the burden of location, with consequences for affordability and for travel-demand forecasts built on stated commuting behavior.
